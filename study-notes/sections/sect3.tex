\section{Life Annuity}
\label{sect:life-annuity}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Apart from insurance coverages, another common type of insurance product
that is offered by life insurers is \emph{life annuity}.  While life insurance
is primarily about insuring against the risk of ``living for too short'', or
dying \rc{\faIcon{skull}} too early (except for those (pure) endowment
insurances), life annuity is about insuring against the risk of ``living for
too long''. ``Living for too long'' is also a risk since, for example, the
retirement savings may not be adequate for maintaining a good quality of life
if the lifetime is unexpectedly long. Life annuity provides \emph{survival}
benefit payments as long as the \defn{annuitant} (we use this term instead of
``life insured'' for annuity products) survives, which yields a stable stream
of incomes for maintaining the quality of life after retirement.

\item Like life insurance, the present value of the benefit payments (PVRV
here) directly contributes to the amount of loss for the insurer. If the lifetime
is longer, more survival benefit payments would need to be made, which
amplifies the insurer's loss. So in \Cref{sect:life-annuity}, we will
investigate the probabilistic behaviours of the present value of benefit
payments, like what we did in \Cref{sect:life-insurance}. The organization of
\Cref{sect:life-annuity} will be (highly) similar to
\Cref{sect:life-insurance}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Basic Life Annuities}
\label{subsect:basic-annuity}
\begin{enumerate}
\item We shall start by studying some basic life annuities: (i) whole life
annuity, (ii) temporary life annuity, (iii) deferred whole life annuity, and
(iv) guaranteed annuity. Like what we did in \Cref{sect:life-insurance}, we
shall explore each kind of life annuity in the following three aspects:
\begin{enumerate}[label={(\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Definition:} meaning of the annuity
\item \emph{Key formulas:} formulas for some key probabilistic quantities of PVRV
\item \emph{Recursive formulas:} formulas used for recursions (to be discussed
after studying the first two aspects for all four kinds of basic life annuities)
\end{enumerate}
Henceforth, we shall assume that the amount of \emph{each} benefit payment is
\(1\) (for discrete life annuities) or the \emph{rate} of benefit
payment is \(1\) (for continuous life annuities), unless otherwise
specified. \begin{note}
We will explain what ``discrete'' and ``continuous'' mean very soon.
\end{note}

Throughout, we shall consider the case where the annuity is purchased when the
annuitant is aged \(x\), so we are interested in the present value at age
\(x\).
\item \textbf{\defn{Whole life annuity}.}
\begin{enumerate}[label={(\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Definition:} Periodic benefit payments are made whenever the annuitant is
alive, for life.

For all kinds of life annuities in \Cref{subsect:basic-annuity}, they
can be broadly classified into two types: (i) discrete and (ii) continuous:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Discrete:} The benefit payments are made at \emph{discrete time
points}. More specifically, we are considering the following two cases (learnt
in STAT2902):
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Due:} Payments are made at the beginning of year.

The general EPV calculation formula introduced in \labelcref{it:gen-epv-fmla}
is still useful for obtaining the summation/integral formula for EPV here. For example, in the continuous case above:
\begin{itemize}
\item ``All possible payment times'' start at time 0 with no end, thus we have
``\(\int_{0}^{\infty}\)''.
\item \brc{Benefit amount} at time \(t\) (amount of payment made in
\([t,t+\dd{t}]\)) is loosely \[
\underbrace{\text{rate}}_{1}\times \underbrace{\text{time length}}_{\dd{t}}
=\brc{\dd{t}}.\]
\item \vc{Discount factor} is \vc{\(v^t\)} at time \(t\).
\item \mgc{Probability of triggering payment} at time \(t\) is the probability
that the annuitant is alive at that time, i.e., \mgc{\(\px[t]{x}\)}.
\end{itemize}

\item \label{it:percentile-trick-annuity} \textbf{Percentile trick.}
The random variable of interest here is the PVRV, denoted by \(Y\). For
many annuity products discussed here, we can write \(Y=h(T_x)\) for some
strictly \emph{increasing} continuous function \(h\). Then, the \emph{percentile trick}
suggests that we actually have
\[
\pi_{p}(Y)=\pi_{p}(h(T_x))=h(\pi_{p}(T_x)).
\]
In words, the \(p\)th percentile of \(h(T_x)\) equals \(h\) evaluated at the
\(p\)th percentile of \(T_x\). This allows us to compute percentiles of PVRV
more efficiently.

\end{enumerate}
\subsection{Variations of Basic Life Annuities}
\label{subsect:variations-annuity}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Like \Cref{sect:life-insurance}, here we will discuss three kinds of
variations of basic life annuities: (i) \(1/m\)thly annuity, (ii) variable
annuity benefits and (iii) \emph{(new!)} complete and apportionable annuities.
We shall explore them in the following three aspects:
\begin{enumerate}[label={(\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Definition:} meaning of the variant
\item \emph{Key formulas:} formulas for some key probabilistic quantities of PVRV
\item \emph{Recursive formulas:} formulas used for recursions
\end{enumerate}
\item \textbf{\defn{\(1/m\)thly annuity}.} \warn{} Here we shall assume that
the amount of each benefit payment is \rc{\(1/m\)} rather than \(1\), so that
the \emph{total} benefit amount in a year is \(1\).
\begin{enumerate}[label={(\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Definition:} Benefit payment is made at the beginning (due case) or
end (immediate case) of each \(1/m\)th of a year in the relevant period,
whenever the annuitant is alive.
\item \emph{Key formulas:} Like the insurance case, the formulas for
\(1/m\)thly annuity can be obtained by modifying the respective formulas for
\emph{discrete} case suitably:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{PVRV:} 
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{(both due and immediate)} Multiply by \(1/m\).
\item \emph{(due)} Change \(K_x+1\to K_x^{\mgc{(m)}}+\mgc{1/m}\) and
\(\ax**{}\to \ax**{}[(m)]\) (recall the meaning of annuity symbol with
superscript ``\((m)\)'' from STAT2902).
\item \emph{(immediate)} Change \(K_x\to K_x^{\mgc{(m)}}\) and \(\ax{}\to\ax{}[(m)]\).
\end{itemize}
\item \emph{EPV:}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Notation:} Put ``\((m)\)'' as the right superscript of
\(\ax**{}\) or \(\ax{}\) in the notation for discrete case.
\item \emph{Formula:}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{(annuity-insurance)} Change \(\Ax{}\to\Ax{}[\mgc{(m)}]\) and \(d\to d^{\mgc{(m)}}\).
\item \emph{(summation)} Still use the general EPV formula in
\labelcref{it:gen-epv-fmla} (remember that the amount of each payment is
\(1/m\) rather than \(1\) \warn{}).
\item \emph{(due-immediate)} Change \(\ax{}\to \ax{}[\mgc{(m)}]\), \(\ax**{}\to
\ax**{}[\mgc{(m)}]\) and \(1\to \mgc{1/m}\) (both \(-1\to \mgc{-1/m}\) and \((1)\Ex[n]{x}\to
\mgc{(1/m)}\Ex[n]{x}\) (if applicable)).
\end{itemize}
\item \emph{Variance:} \emph{(due only)} Change \(\Ax{}\to \Ax{}[\mgc{(m)}]\).
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\item \label{it:compare-annuity-EPV-by-freq} Like the insurance case,
\(1/m\)thly annuity serves as a ``middle ground'' between discrete annuity and
continuous annuity. Note that in any of these three types of annuity, the total
benefit amount in a year is \(1\).  Their difference lies in the \emph{payment
frequency}, with the following order:
\(\text{discrete}\le\text{\(1/m\)thly}\le\text{continuous}\).

In view of this, we are then interested to know how the EPVs vary as the
payment frequency changes. The results are summarized below:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Due:} \(\ax**{x}[(m)]\) \emph{decreases} in \(m\).

\begin{intuition}
As \(m\) increases, the overall payment time in each year becomes \emph{later},
resulting in a \emph{larger} discounting, hence \emph{smaller} EPV.
\end{intuition}
\item \emph{Immediate:} \(\ax{x}[(m)]\) \emph{increases} in \(m\).

\begin{intuition}
As \(m\) increases, the overall payment time in each year becomes \emph{earlier},
resulting in a \emph{smaller} discounting, hence \emph{larger} EPV.
\end{intuition}
\item \emph{Comparison between different kinds of annuity:}
\(\ax{x}\le\ax{x}[(m)]\le\ax*{x}\le\ax**{x}[(m)]\le\ax**{x}\).

\begin{intuition}
The order of the overall payment time in each year for these kinds of annuities
is:
\[
\underset{\text{(latest)}}{\text{immediate}}\ge\text{\(1/m\)thly immediate}
\ge\text{continuous}\ge\text{\(1/m\)thly due}\ge \underset{\text{(earliest)}}{\text{due}}.
\]
Hence the reverse order applies for the EPVs.
\end{intuition}
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{\defn{Geometrically increasing annuity}.}
\begin{enumerate}[label={(\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Definition:} The benefit amount (if payable) is \((1+j)^{k}\) at
time \(k\), for each \(k=0,1,\dotsc\).
\item \emph{Key formulas:} We use geometrically increasing \emph{\(n\)-year
term life insurance} as an example. Like the insurance case in
\labelcref{it:geometric-insur}, we need to introduce an auxiliary interest rate
\(i^*=(i-j)/(1+j)\):
\[
\text{EPV}=\text{\(\ax**{\endowxn}\) @ \(i^*\)}\overset{(i=j,\, n\to\infty)}{\to} 1+e_x.
\]
\begin{pf}
Firstly, note that
\[
\text{EPV}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(1+j)^{k}v^{k}\px[k]{x}
=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(1+i^*)^{-k}\px[k]{x}
=\text{\(\ax**{\endowxn}\) @ \(i^*\)}.
\]
When \(i=j\), we have \(i^*=0\), so
\[
\text{\(\ax**{\endowxn}\) @ \(i^*\)}
\overset{(n\to \infty)}{\to}
\ax**{x}^{\text{@\(i^*=0\)}}
=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}1^k\px[k]{x}
=1+\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\px[k]{x}}_{e_x}.
\]
\end{pf}
\end{enumerate}
\item \textbf{\defn{Complete and apportionable annuities}.}
\begin{enumerate}[label={(\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Definition:}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Complete:} Change \(\displaystyle \ax{}[(m)]\to \frac{\delta}{i^{(m)}}\times \ax*{}\).

\begin{note}
It is only applicable to \(1/m\)thly life annuity-\underline{immediate}.
\end{note}
\item \emph{Apportionable:} Change \(\displaystyle \ax**{}[(m)]\to \frac{\delta}{d^{(m)}}\times \ax*{}\).

\begin{note}
It is only applicable to \(1/m\)thly life annuity-\underline{due}.
\end{note}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Relating \(\bar{a}\), \(\ddot{a}\) and \(\ddot{a}^{(m)}\)}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Like the insurance case, there are two kinds of formulas that relate
\(\ax*{}\), \(\ax**{}\) and \(\ax**{}[(m)]\):
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{UDD:} Formulas that work under the UDD assumption
\item \emph{Woolhouse's formulas:} \underline{Approximation} formulas that work in general
\end{itemize}
Again such relationship is of interest since in many tables (e.g., SOA FAM-L
table and AM92 table), only the values of ``\(\ax**{}\)''s are given, but not
the ``\(\ax**{}[(m)]\)''s and ``\(\ax*{}\)''s.
\item \label{it:udd-relate-annuity} \textbf{UDD.} The UDD formulas can be
organized into two groups:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{\(\ax**{}\overset{\text{UDD}}{\to}\ax**{}[(m)]\):}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Annuity type&UDD formula \\
\midrule
Whole life&\(\ax**{x}[(m)]=\alpha(m)\ax**{x}\rc{-}\beta(m)\) \\
Temporary life&\(\ax**{\endowxn}[(m)]=\alpha(m)\ax**{\endowxn}\rc{-}\beta(m)\rc{(1-\Ex[n]{x})}\) \\
Deferred whole life&\(\ax**[n|]{x}[(m)]=\alpha(m)\ax**[n|]{x}\rc{-}\beta(m)\rc{\Ex[n]{x}}\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
We have \(\displaystyle \alpha(m)=\frac{id}{i^{(m)}d^{(m)}}\) and
\(\displaystyle \beta(m)=\frac{i-i^{(m)}}{i^{(m)}d^{(m)}}\).
\item \emph{\(\ax**{}\overset{\text{UDD}}{\to}\ax*{}\):} Let \(m\to\infty\),
which changes \(\ax**{}[(m)]\to\ax*{}\), in the UDD formulas for
\(\ax**{}\overset{\text{UDD}}{\to}\ax**{}[(m)]\). More specifically, make the
following changes on the UDD formulas for
\(\ax**{}\overset{\text{UDD}}{\to}\ax**{}[(m)]\):
\begin{itemize}
\item \(\ax**{}[(m)]\to \ax*{}\).
\item \(\displaystyle \alpha(m)\to \alpha(\infty)=\frac{id}{\mgc{\delta^2}}\).
\item \(\displaystyle \beta(m)\to \beta(\infty)=\frac{i-\mgc{\delta}}{\mgc{\delta^2}}\).
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
\begin{note}
Values of \(\alpha(m)\) and \(\beta(m)\) at \(i=0.05\) for some \(m\)
(including \(m=\infty\)) can be found in the SOA FAM-L table.
\end{note}
\item \label{it:woolhouse} \textbf{\defn{Woolhouse's formula}.} Woolhouse's
formula is an approximation formula for \(\ax**{}\to\ax**{}[(m)]\) and
\(\ax**{}\to\ax*{}\).  Like the claims acceleration approach for the insurance
case, the advantage \faIcon[regular]{thumbs-up} of using Woolhouse's formula is
that the UDD assumption is not needed, but the obvious disadvantage
\faIcon[regular]{thumbs-down} is that the formula only serves for
approximation.

There are two forms of Woolhouse's formula, identified by the number of terms
involved. Both are for \(\ax**{x}\overset{\approx}{\to}\ax**{x}[(m)]\).
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{3-term formula:} \(\displaystyle \boxed{\ax**{x}[(m)]\approx
\ax**{x}-\frac{m-1}{2m}-\frac{m^{2}-1}{12m^{2}}(\mu_x+\delta)}\). \begin{note}
This is available in the SOA FAM-L table.
\end{note}
\item \emph{2-term formula:} Drop the last term in the 3-term formula, i.e.,
\(\displaystyle \boxed{\ax**{x}[(m)]\approx\ax**{x}-\frac{m-1}{2m}}\).
\end{itemize}
\begin{remark}
\item For the 3-term formula, if \(\mu_x\) is not available, we can approximate
it by \(\displaystyle \boxed{\mu_x\approx
-\frac{1}{2}\ln\qty(\frac{\lx{x+1}}{\lx{x-1}})}\). This approximation comes from:
\[
\frac{\lx{x+1}}{\lx{x-1}}=\px[2]{x-1}=\exp\qty(-\int_{x-1}^{x+1}\mu_{s}\dd{s})
\;\rc{\approx}\; e^{-2\mu_x}.
\]
\item For \(\ax**{\endowxn}\overset{\approx}{\to}\ax**{\endowxn}[(m)]\)
(temporary life) and \(\ax**[n|]{x}\overset{\approx}{\to}\ax**[n|]{x}[(m)]\)
(deferred whole life), the key \faIcon{key} trick is to express the temporary
life annuity and deferred whole life annuity EPVs in terms of the whole life
annuity EPVs, so that Woolhouse's formula can be applied:
\begin{align*}
\ax**{\endowxn}[(m)]&=\underbrace{\ax**{x}[(m)]}_{\mathclap{\text{apply Woolhouse}}}
-\Ex[n]{x}\times \underbrace{\ax**{x+n}[(m)]}_{\mathclap{\text{apply Woolhouse}}}=\dotsb
=\text{expression in terms of \(\ax**{\endowxn}\)}, \\
\ax**[n|]{x}[(m)]&=\Ex[n]{x}\times \underbrace{\ax**{x+n}[(m)]}_{\mathclap{\text{apply Woolhouse}}}=\dotsb
=\text{expression in terms of \(\ax**[n|]{x}\)}.
\end{align*}
\item For \(\ax**{x}\overset{\approx}{\to}\ax*{x}\), again we would let
\(m\to\infty\), and then the 3-term formula would become
\[
\boxed{\ax*{x}\approx \ax**{x}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{12}(\mu_x+\delta)}.
\]
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Special case:} Since
\(\eringx{x}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\px[t]{x}\dd{t}=\ax*{x}\text{ with \(i=0\)}\),
the approximation formula above allows us to approximate \(\eringx{x}\):
\[
\boxed{\eringx{x}\approx}\underbrace{(\text{\(\ax**{x}\) @ \(i=0\)})}_{\rc{1+}e_x}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{12}(\mu_x+0)
=\boxed{e_x\rc{+}\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{12}\mu_x}.
\]
\end{itemize}
\item \emph{(If you are interested)} For more details about how the Woolhouse's
formula is derived, see \textcite{dickson2019actuarial}.
\item \emph{(Not commonly used)} Applying annuity-insurance formula, we can
obtain yet another formula for \(\Ax{x}\overset{\approx }{\to}\Ax{x}[(m)]\):
\begin{align*}
\Ax{x}[(m)]&=1-d^{(m)}\ax**{x}[(m)] \\
&\approx 1-d^{(m)}\qty(\ax**{x}-\frac{m-1}{2m}-\frac{m^{2}-1}{12m^{2}}(\mu_x+\delta)) &\text{(Woolhouse)} \\
&=1-d^{(m)}\qty(\frac{1-\Ax{x}}{d}-\frac{m-1}{2m}-\frac{m^{2}-1}{12m^{2}}(\mu_x+\delta)).
\end{align*}

\end{remark}
\end{enumerate}
