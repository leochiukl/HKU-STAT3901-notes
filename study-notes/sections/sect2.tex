\section{Life Insurance}
\label{sect:life-insurance}
\begin{enumerate}
\item As mentioned at the beginning of \Cref{sect:surv-models}, a motivation
for studying survival model is that the lifetime of the insured life impacts
greatly the present value of the benefit payment, hence the loss for the
insurer. After studying survival models in \Cref{sect:surv-models} and the
probabilistic behaviours of lifetime in various models, let us investigate how
the present value of the benefit payment would behave in
\Cref{sect:life-insurance}. Such present value is called the \defn{present
value random variable} (PVRV). It is a random variable since it is a function
of the lifetime, which is a random variable.

\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Basic Insurance Coverages}
\label{subsect:basic-insurance}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Let us start with studying some basic kinds of insurance coverages: (i)
whole life insurance, (ii) term life insurance, (iii) pure endowment, (iv)
endowment insurance, and (v) deferred whole life insurance. They will serve as
our basic building blocks for all the discussions in
\Cref{sect:life-insurance}. To guide our discussions here, for each kind of
insurance coverage, we shall explore it in the following three aspects:
\begin{enumerate}[label={(\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Definition:} meaning of the insurance coverage
\item \emph{Key formulas:} formulas for some key probabilistic quantities of PVRV
\item \emph{Recursive formulas:} formulas used for recursions (to be discussed
after studying the first two aspects for all five kinds of basic insurance
coverages)
\end{enumerate}
For convenience, henceforth we shall assume that the amount of benefit payment
is always \(1\), unless otherwise specified. If the actual benefit amount is
not \(1\), then we can just multiply the PVRV by the actual benefit amount and
adjust the formulas accordingly. Throughout, we shall consider the case where
the insurance coverage is purchased when the life insured is aged \(x\), so we
are interested in the present value at age \(x\).

\item \textbf{\defn{Whole life insurance}.}
\begin{enumerate}[label={(\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Definition:} A death benefit (DB) is paid upon the death \faIcon{skull}
of the life insured (lifetime coverage: payable regardless of when the insured
life dies).


For all kinds of insurance coverages in \Cref{subsect:basic-insurance}, they
can be broadly classified into two types: (i) discrete and (ii) continuous:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Discrete:} The benefit payment is paid \emph{at the end of the year} of death.
\item \emph{Continuous:} The benefit payment is paid \emph{at the moment} of death.
\end{itemize}
More specifically, if we start counting time at the age \(x\) of the life
insured:
\begin{itemize}
\item The end of the year of death is at time \(K_x+1\).
\item The moment of death is at time \(T_x\).
\end{itemize}
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}

\fill[] (0,0) circle [radius=0.05]

\fill[] (6,0) circle [radius=0.05]

\item \emph{Key formulas:}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
&Discrete&Continuous \\
\midrule
PVRV&\(v^{K_x+1}\)&\(v^{T_x}\) \\
EPV (mean of PVRV)&\(\displaystyle \Ax{x}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\vc{v^{k+1}}\mgc{\px[k]{x}\qx[]{x+k}}\)&
\(\displaystyle \Ax*{x}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\vc{e^{-\delta t}}\mgc{\px[t]{x}\mu_{x+t}\dd{t}}\)
\\
Second moment&\(\displaystyle \Ax[][2]{x}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\vc{v^{\blc{2}(k+1)}}\mgc{\px[k]{x}\qx[]{x+k}}\)&\(
\displaystyle \Ax*[][2]{x}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\vc{e^{-\blc{2}\delta t}}\mgc{\px[t]{x}\mu_{x+t}\dd{t}}
\) \\
Variance&\(\Ax[][2]{x}-\Ax{x}^{2}\)&\(\Ax*[][2]{x}-\Ax*{x}^{2}\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{remark}
\item The second moment is actually just the EPV evaluated at double force of
interest, i.e., \(\delta\to 2\delta\) or \(v\to v^2\).
\item EPV stands for \defn{expected present value}. Sometimes the term
\defn{actuarial present value} (APV) is used instead.
\end{remark}
\end{enumerate}
\item \label{it:gen-epv-fmla} \ystar{} \textbf{Important note.} Here we can
observe that the EPV formulas take a similar ``form'' for both discrete and
continuous cases: Both are ``summing'' products of terms. Generally speaking,
we have the following formula for calculating EPV:
\[
\text{EPV}=\underset{\text{all possible payment times}}{\sum\text{ or }\int}\brc{\text{benefit amount}}\times\vc{\text{discount factor}}\times\mgc{\text{prob.\ of triggering payment}}.
\]
For example, in the continuous case above:
\begin{itemize}
\item ``All possible payment times'' start at time 0 with no end, thus we have
``\(\int_{0}^{\infty}\)''.
\item \brc{Benefit amount} is \(1\), so we do not see it in the formula.
\item \vc{Discount factor} is \vc{\(v^t\)} at time \(t\).
\item \mgc{Probability of triggering payment} at time \(t\) is loosely
\[\text{probability that \(T_x\in [t, t+\dd{t}]\)}=\underbrace{\mgc{\px[t]{x}}}_{\text{survive \(t\) yrs.}}\times 
\underbrace{\mgc{\mu_{x+t}\dd{t}}}_{\text{die in the subsequent \(\dd{t}\) yrs. (instantaneously)}}.\]
\end{itemize}

Once you understand how this formula works, there is no need to ``memorize''
the EPV formula for every single kind of insurance product
\faIcon[regular]{thumbs-up}.  In fact, this formula is still applicable for
\emph{life annuities} (to be discussed in \Cref{sect:life-annuity}) and even in
STAT3909!

\item \textbf{\defn{Term life insurance}.}
\begin{enumerate}[label={(\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Definition:} A DB is paid upon the death of the life insured,
provided that the death occurs within the policy term, say \(n\) years.

\item \emph{Key formulas:}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
&Discrete&Continuous \\
\midrule
PVRV&\(\begin{cases}
v^{K_x+1}&\text{if \(K_x=0,1,\dotsc,n-1\)}, \\
0&\text{if \(K_x=n,n+1,\dotsc\)}
\end{cases}
\)&\(
\begin{cases}
v^{T_x}&\text{if \(T_x\le n\)}, \\
0&\text{if \(T_x>n\)}
\end{cases}
\) \\
EPV&\(\displaystyle \Ax{\termxn}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\vc{v^{k+1}}\mgc{\px[k]{x}\qx[]{x+k}}\)&
\(\displaystyle \Ax*{\termxn}=\int_{0}^{n}\vc{e^{-\delta t}}\mgc{\px[t]{x}\mu_{x+t}\dd{t}}\)
\\
Second moment&\(\displaystyle \Ax[][2]{\termxn}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\vc{v^{\blc{2}(k+1)}}\mgc{\px[k]{x}\qx[]{x+k}}\)&\(
\displaystyle \Ax*[][2]{\termxn}=\int_{0}^{n}\vc{e^{-\blc{2}\delta t}}\mgc{\px[t]{x}\mu_{x+t}\dd{t}}
\) \\
Variance&\(\Ax[][2]{\termxn}-(\Ax{\termxn})^{2}\)&\(\Ax*[][2]{\termxn}-(\Ax*{\termxn})^{2}\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{note}
The ``\(1\)'' on top of \(x\) suggests that the life aged \(x\) is the
\underline{1}st thing to be ``gone'' (dead \faIcon{skull}), earlier than the
elapse of the \(n\)-year term, for the benefit to be triggered. More details
about this kind of notation will be discussed in STAT3909.
\end{note}
\end{enumerate}
\item \textbf{\defn{Pure endowment}.}
\begin{enumerate}[label={(\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Definition:} A \emph{survival benefit} is paid at the end of the
policy term (say \(n\) years) if the life insured is still alive \faIcon{child}
at that time.

\begin{note}
There is no distinction of ``continuous'' and ``discrete'' for pure endowment,
because the only possible payment time is always precisely at the end of the
policy term.
\end{note}

\item \emph{Key formulas:}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
&Formula\\
\midrule
PVRV&\(\begin{cases}
0&\text{if \(T_x<n\)}, \\
v^n&\text{if \(T_x\ge n\)}
\end{cases}
\) \\
\midrule
EPV&\(\displaystyle \Ax{\pureendowxn}=\Ex[n]{x}=\vc{v^{n}}\mgc{\px[n]{x}}\)\\
\midrule
Second moment&\(\displaystyle \Ax[][2]{\pureendowxn}=\Ex[n][2]{x}=\vc{v^{2n}}\px[n]{x}=v^{n}\Ex[n]{x}\) \\
\midrule
Variance&\(\Ex[n][2]{x}-(\Ex[n]{x})^{2}=v^{2n}\times \px[n]{x}\times \qx[n]{x}\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{remark}
\item The ``\(1\)'' on top of \(\angl{n}\) suggests that the \(n\)-year term is the
\underline{1}st thing to be ``gone'' (elapsed), earlier than death
\faIcon{skull} of the life aged \(x\), for the benefit to be triggered. Again,
more details about this kind of notation will be discussed in STAT3909.
\item The notation for EPV is \underline{not} ``\(\Ax*{\pureendowxn}\)''!
\end{remark}
\end{enumerate}
\item \textbf{\defn{Endowment insurance}.}
\begin{enumerate}[label={(\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Definition:} A combination of a term life insurance and a pure
endowment of the same term (say \(n\)-year).

\begin{note}
In general, unless otherwise specified, we shall assume that the term life
insurance and pure endowment have the same benefit amount. If they have
different benefit amounts, then such insurance is said to be a
\underline{special} endowment insurance.
\end{note}
\item \emph{Key formulas:}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
&Discrete&Continuous \\
\midrule
PVRV&\(v^{(K_x+1)\wedge n}=\begin{cases}
v^{K_x+1}&\text{if \(K_x=0,1,\dotsc,n-1\)}, \\
v^{n}&\text{if \(K_x=n,n+1,\dotsc\)}
\end{cases}
\)&\(v^{T_x\wedge n}=
\begin{cases}
v^{T_x}&\text{if \(T_x\le n\)}, \\
v^{n}&\text{if \(T_x>n\)}
\end{cases}
\) \\
\midrule
EPV&\(\Ax{\endowxn}=\Ax{\termxn}+\Ax{\pureendowxn}\)&
\(\Ax*{\endowxn}=\Ax*{\termxn}+\Ax{\pureendowxn}\)
\\
\midrule
Second moment&\(\Ax[][2]{\endowxn}=\Ax[][2]{\termxn}+\Ax[][2]{\pureendowxn}\)&\(
\Ax*[][2]{\endowxn}=\Ax*[][2]{\termxn}+\Ax[][2]{\pureendowxn}
\) \\
\midrule
Variance&\makecell[l]{\(\Ax[][2]{\endowxn}-(\Ax{\endowxn})^{2}\) or\\
\(\vari{Z_{\mathrm{term}}}+\vari{Z_{\mathrm{PE}}}
-2\underbrace{\Ax{\termxn}\Ax{\pureendowxn}}_{\mathclap{\cov{Z_{\mathrm{term}}}{Z_{\mathrm{PE}}}}}\)
}&
\makecell[l]{
\(\Ax*[][2]{\endowxn}-(\Ax*{\endowxn})^{2}\) or\\
\(\vari{Z_{\mathrm{term}}}+\vari{Z_{\mathrm{PE}}}
-2\underbrace{\Ax*{\termxn}\Ax{\pureendowxn}}_{\mathclap{\cov{Z_{\mathrm{term}}}{Z_{\mathrm{PE}}}}}\)
} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{note}
\(Z_{\mathrm{term}}\) and \(Z_{\mathrm{PE}}\) denote the PVRV's for
(\(n\)-year) term life insurance and pure endowment respectively. Note that the
PVRV for endowment insurance is \(Z_{\mathrm{term}}+Z_{\mathrm{PE}}\).
\end{note}
\end{enumerate}
\item \textbf{\defn{Deferred whole life insurance}.}
\begin{enumerate}[label={(\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Definition:} A DB is paid upon the death of the life insured,
provided that the death occurs after the deferred period (e.g., \(n\) years).
\item \emph{Key formulas:}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
&Discrete&Continuous \\
\midrule
PVRV&\(\begin{cases}
0&\text{if \(K_x=0,1,\dotsc,n-1\)}, \\
v^{K_x+1}&\text{if \(K_x=n,n+1,\dotsc\)}
\end{cases}
\)&\(
\begin{cases}
0&\text{if \(T_x\le n\)}, \\
v^{T_x}&\text{if \(T_x>n\)}
\end{cases}
\) \\
\midrule
EPV&\(\Ax[n|]{x}=\Ax{x}-\Ax{\termxn}=\Ex[n]{x}\Ax{x+n}\)&
\(\Ax*[n|]{x}=\Ax*{x}-\Ax*{\termxn}=\Ex[n]{x}\Ax*{x+n}\)
\\
\midrule \\

For the variance, we can also use a similar approach as the one for endowment
insurance. We first let \(Z_{\mathrm{WL}}\), \(Z_{\mathrm{term}}\), and
\(Z_{\mathrm{deferred}}\) denote the PVRV's for whole life insurance,
\(n\)-year term life insurance, and \(n\)-year deferred whole life insurance
respectively. Then, we have
\(Z_{\mathrm{WL}}=Z_{\mathrm{term}}+Z_{\mathrm{deferred}}\), thus
\begin{align*}
\vari{Z_{\mathrm{WL}}}&=\vari{Z_{\mathrm{term}}}+\vari{Z_{\mathrm{deferred}}}+2\cov{Z_{\mathrm{term}}}{Z_{\mathrm{deferred}}} \\
&=\begin{cases}
\vari{Z_{\mathrm{term}}}+\vari{Z_{\mathrm{deferred}}}-2\Ax{\termxn}\times \Ax[n|]{x}&
\text{for discrete case}, \\
\vari{Z_{\mathrm{term}}}+\vari{Z_{\mathrm{deferred}}}-2\Ax*{\termxn}\times \Ax*[n|]{x}&
\text{for continuous case}.
\end{cases}
\end{align*}
If the variances \(\vari{Z_{\mathrm{WL}}}\) and \(\vari{Z_{\mathrm{term}}}\)
are known, we can then deduce the desired variance \(\vari{Z_{\mathrm{deferred}}}\).
\end{enumerate}

\item Apart from EPV, second moment, and variance,
another probabilistic quantity of PVRV that is of interest is the
\emph{percentile}. Recall that the \emph{\(p\)th percentile} of a random variable
\(X\) is the value \(c\) that makes \(\prob{X\le c}=p\).\footnote{This is
actually a simplified definition that works for some sufficiently nice random
variable only. But this definition is sufficient for our purpose here.} Here we
denote the \(p\)th percentile of \(X\) by \(\pi_{p}(X)\).

\item \label{it:percentile-trick-insurance} \textbf{Percentile trick.} In our case here,
the random variable of interest is the PVRV, and we denote it by \(Z\). For
many insurance coverages discussed here, we can write \(Z=g(T_x)\) for some
strictly \emph{decreasing} continuous function \(g\). Then, the \emph{percentile trick}
suggests that we actually have
\[
\pi_{p}(Z)=\pi_{p}(g(T_x))=g(\pi_{\rc{1-p}}(T_x)).
\]
In words, the \(p\)th percentile of \(g(T_x)\) equals \(g\) evaluated at the
(\rc{\(1-p\)})th percentile of \(T_x\). Because percentiles of \(T_x\) are
usually easier to find, this trick can allow us to compute percentiles of PVRV
more efficiently.
\end{enumerate}
\subsection{Variations of Basic Insurance Coverages}
\label{subsect:variations-insurance}
\begin{enumerate}
\item We have mentioned at the beginning of \Cref{subsect:basic-insurance} that
the basic insurance coverages studied in \Cref{subsect:basic-insurance} will
serve as our building blocks. Now, let us those building blocks to construct
some more advanced insurance coverages, by introducing some variants to those
basic coverages. Here, we shall discuss two kinds of variants: (i) \(1/m\)thly
insurance and (ii) variable insurance benefits. Likewise, we shall explore each
of them in the following three aspects:
\begin{enumerate}[label={(\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Definition:} meaning of the variant
\item \emph{Key formulas:} formulas for some key probabilistic quantities of PVRV
\item \emph{Recursive formulas:} formulas used for recursions
\end{enumerate}

\item \textbf{\defn{\(1/m\)thly insurance}.}
\begin{enumerate}[label={(\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Definition:} A DB is paid at the end of the \(1/m\)th year of
death.  (For example, if \(m=12\), then a DB is paid at the end of the
\emph{month} (``\(1/12\)th year'') of death.)

The \(1/m\)thly insurance serves as a middle ground between ``discrete'' and
``continuous''.

\item \emph{Key formulas:} The formulas for \(1/m\)thly insurance can generally
be obtained by modifying the respective formulas for \emph{discrete} case
suitably. Before discussing the modifications, we first need to introduce a
preliminary concept, which serves as a middle ground between \(K_x\) and \(T_x\).

The \defn{\(1/m\)thly curtate future lifetime random variable} \(K_x^{(m)}\)
is defined by \(K_x^{(m)}=\frac{1}{m}\lfloor mT_x\rfloor\). In words,
\(K_x^{(m)}\) is \(T_x\) \emph{rounded down to the lower \(1/m\)th of a year}.

For example, \(K_x^{(2)}\) is \(T_x\) rounded down to the lower half of a year:

node[below] {\(T_x\)};
\fill[] (8,0) circle [radius=0.05]
node[below right] {\(K_x+1\)};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
The pmf of \(K_x^{(m)}\) is given by
\(\prob{K_x^{(m)}=\frac{k}{m}}=\px[\frac{k}{m}]{x}\times \qx[\frac{1}{m}]{x+\frac{k}{m}}\)
for each \(k=0,1,\dotsc\).


Using the random variable \(K_x^{(m)}\), we can then specify the modifications
needed for obtaining the formulas applicable to \(1/m\)thly insurance.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{PVRV:} Replace \(K_x+1\) by \(K_x^{\mgc{(m)}}+\mgc{1/m}\).

Example (whole life insurance): \(v^{K_x+1}\to v^{K_x^{\mgc{(m)}}+\mgc{1/m}}\).
\item \emph{EPV:}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Notation:} Put ``\((m)\)'' as the right subscript of \(A\) in the
notation for discrete case.
\item \emph{Formula:} Still use the general EPV calculation formula in
\labelcref{it:gen-epv-fmla}.
\end{itemize}

Example (\(n\)-year term life insurance):
\[
\Ax{}[(m)]{}_{\termxn}=\sum_{k=0}^{\rc{mn-1}}v^{\frac{k+1}{m}}\px[\frac{k}{m}]{x}\qx[\frac{1}{m}]{x+\frac{k}{m}}
=v^{\frac{1}{m}}\qx[\frac{1}{m}]{x}+v^{\frac{2}{m}}\px[\frac{1}{m}]{x}\qx[\frac{1}{m}]{x+\frac{1}{m}}
+\dotsb
+v^{n}\px[\frac{mn-1}{m}]{x}\qx[\frac{1}{m}]{x+\frac{mn-1}{m}}.
\]
\end{itemize}
\item \emph{Recursive formulas:} Again the recursive formulas can be obtained
by modifying the ones for discrete case in \labelcref{it:insurance-recursions}
suitably. Let us use whole life insurance (\(\Ax{x}[(m)]\)) as an example:
\[
\Ax{x}[\mgc{(m)}]=\Ax{}[\mgc{(m)}]{}_{\termxn}+\Ex[n]{x}\Ax{x+n}[\mgc{(m)}]
\overset{(n=\mgc{\frac{1}{m}})}{=}v^{\mgc{\frac{1}{m}}}\qx[\mgc{\frac{1}{m}}]{x}+v^{\mgc{\frac{1}{m}}}\px[\mgc{\frac{1}{m}}]{x}\Ax{x+\mgc{\frac{1}{m}}}.
\]

\end{center}
\end{enumerate}
\item \label{it:var-benefit-insurance} \textbf{Variable insurance benefits.}
\begin{enumerate}[label={(\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Definition:} This variant refers generally to any kind of insurance
coverage where the amount of DB can differ for different payment time points.

\begin{note}
In this case, the amount of DB may no longer stay at the constant \(1\) anymore.
\end{note}
\item \emph{Key formulas:}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{EPV:} Use the general EPV formula in \labelcref{it:gen-epv-fmla}.
\item \emph{Second moment:} Still use the general EPV formula in
\labelcref{it:gen-epv-fmla}, but square \emph{both} benefit amount \emph{and}
discount factor.
\item \emph{Variance:} Still use the standard formula:
\(\text{Variance}=\text{Second moment}-\text{EPV}^{2}\) (nothing special).
\end{itemize}

\item\label{it:geometric-insur} \textbf{\defn{Geometrically increasing insurance}.}
\begin{enumerate}[label={(\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Definition:} \emph{(only applicable to the discrete case)} The
amount of DB paid at time \rc{\(k+1\)} (if payable) is
\((1+j)^{\rc{k}}\) for any \(k=0,1,\dotsc\).
\begin{note}
With this definition, the amount of DB at time \(1\) (the first possible DB) is
\(1\).
\end{note}
\item \emph{Key formulas: }Let us use geometrically increasing \emph{\(n\)-year
term life insurance} as an example.
\[
\text{EPV}=\frac{1}{1+j}A_{x:\angl{n}i^{*}}\overset{(i=j)}{=}\frac{1}{1+j}\qx[n]{x}
\]
where \(i^*=(i-j)/(1+j)\).

\begin{pf}
Note that
\[
\text{EPV}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(1+j)^{k}v^{k+1}\px[k]{x}\qx{x+k}
=\frac{1}{1+j}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\underbrace{\qty(\frac{1+i}{1+j})^{-(k+1)}}_{\vc{(1+i^{*})^{-(k+1)}}}\mgc{\px[k]{x}\qx{x+k}},
\]
and that when \(i^*=0\), we have
\(A_{x:\angl{n}i^{*}}=\expv{\indicset{T_x<n}}=\qx[n]{x}\), where
\(\indicset{\cdot}\) denotes the indicator function.
\end{pf}
\end{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Incorporating selection.} With selection at age \(x\), all the
previous ``\(A\)'' symbols and formulas can still be used, after replacing
``\(x\)'' by \([x]\) and simplifying ``\([x]+t\)'' to ``\(x+t\)'' if \(t\ge
\underset{\text{(select period)}}{d}\).
\end{enumerate}
\subsection{Relating \(\bar{A}\), \(A\), and \(A^{(m)}\)}
\begin{enumerate}
\item In \Cref{subsect:basic-insurance,subsect:variations-insurance}, we have
studied formulas for EPVs of continuous (``\(\Ax*{}\)''), discrete
(``\(\Ax{}\)'') and \(1/m\)thly (``\(\Ax{}[(m)]\)'') insurances. Here, we would
like to investigate their relationship. This is of interest since in many
tables (like the SOA FAM-L table or the AM92 table), only the values of
discrete EPVs (those ``\(\Ax{}\)s'') can be found, but not the values of
continuous EPVs (those ``\(\Ax*{}\)''s) or \(1/m\)thly EPVs (those
``\(\Ax{}[(m)]\)''s).

\item Here we will discuss two kinds of formulas that relate these EPVs:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{UDD:} Formulas that work under the UDD assumption
\item \emph{Claims acceleration approach:} \underline{Approximation} formulas that work in general
\end{itemize}

\item \label{it:udd-relate-insurance} \textbf{UDD.} We can organize the UDD
formulas into three groups:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{\(\Ax{}\overset{\text{UDD}}{\to}\Ax*{}\):} The common feature
shared by all the formulas here is that the adjustment factor \vc{\(i/\delta\)}
is multiplied:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Type&Formula \\
\midrule
Whole life&\(\displaystyle \Ax*{x}=\vc{\frac{i}{\delta}}\Ax{x}\) \\
Term life&\(\displaystyle \Ax*{\termxn}=\vc{\frac{i}{\delta}}\Ax{\termxn}\) \\
Deferred whole life&\(\displaystyle \Ax*[n|]{x}=\vc{\frac{i}{\delta}}\Ax[n|]{x}\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{warning}
We do \underline{not} have ``\(\displaystyle \Ax*{\endowxn}=\frac{i}{\delta}\Ax{\endowxn}\)''.
The correct formula for the endowment insurance should be
\(\displaystyle \Ax*{\endowxn}=\frac{i}{\delta}\Ax{\termxn}
+\underbrace{\Ax{\pureendowxn}}_{\mathclap{\text{no \(i/\delta\)!}}}.\)
\end{warning}
\item \emph{\(\Ax{}\overset{\text{UDD}}{\to}\Ax{}[(m)]\):} Change \(i/\delta\to
i/\vc{i^{(m)}}\) and \(\Ax*{}\to \vc{\Ax{}[(m)]}\) in the formulas for
\(\Ax{}\overset{\text{UDD}}{\to}\Ax*{}\).
\item \emph{\(\Ax[][2]{}\overset{\text{UDD}}{\to}\Ax*[][2]{}\):} Change
\(i/\delta\to \vc{i^{*}/\delta^*}\) and \(\Ax{}\to \vc{\Ax[][2]{}}\) in the
formulas for \(\Ax{}\overset{\text{UDD}}{\to}\Ax*{}\),
where \(\delta^*=2\delta\) and \(i^*=2i+i^2\) is the effective interest rate
equivalent to the double force of interest \(2\delta\).
\end{itemize}
\item \label{it:claims-acc} \textbf{\defn{Claims acceleration approach}.} This
approach gives us \emph{approximation} formulas for \(\Ax{}\to\Ax*{}\) and
\(\Ax{}\to\Ax{}[(m)]\). The pro \faIcon[regular]{thumbs-up} of this approach is
that it does not require the UDD assumption, but the con
\faIcon[regular]{thumbs-down} is that it is only an \emph{approximation}! The
formulas are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{\(\Ax{}\overset{\approx}{\to}\Ax{}[(m)]\):} Change \(i/i^{(m)}\to
\vc{(1+i)^{(m-1)/2m}}\) and replace ``\(=\)'' by ``\(\approx\)'' in the
formulas for \(\Ax{}\overset{\text{UDD}}{\to}\Ax{}[(m)]\).

\begin{note}
The heuristic idea behind is that for \(1/m\)thly insurances, the average
payment time of DB is the \(\frac{m+1}{2m}\)th of the year of death under UDD,
which is \(1-\frac{m+1}{2m}=\frac{m-1}{2m}\) years earlier than the end of the
year of death for the discrete case. Hence, multiplying
\vc{\((1+i)^{(m-1)/2m}\)} is an attempt to ``bring the DB earlier'' (or
\underline{accelerate} the \underline{claim} payment) through reducing the
magnitude of discounting.
\end{note}
\item \emph{\(\Ax{}\overset{\approx}{\to}\Ax*{}\):} Let \(m\to\infty\), i.e.,
change \((1+i)^{(m-1)/2m}\to (1+i)^{1/2}\), in the formulas for
\(\Ax{}\overset{\approx}{\to}\Ax{}[(m)]\).
\end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
